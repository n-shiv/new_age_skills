# Empathy — Quick Revision Notes

## 1. What is Empathy?
- Understand—or ask—what another person may be **thinking, feeling, and caring about**.
- Consider their existing knowledge, opinions, concerns, and what is at stake.
- Tailor communication to their perspective.
- Empathy is **feeling with people**; sympathy can create distance.

## 2. Empathy Mindset: “Over There” vs. “Over Here”
| Mindset | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Over here** | Own agenda, goals, timelines, needs, and inner dialogue |
| **Over there** | Curiosity about the other person’s thoughts, feelings, priorities, and perspective |

Effective communication starts by understanding where the other person is coming from.

## 3. How to Demonstrate Empathy
- Ask how the person is feeling; don’t assume.
- Give them time and space to express concerns.
- Listen attentively and stay present.
- Make room for their agenda, not only your own.
- Pause before meetings, take a breath, and approach with an open mind.

### Active Listening Behaviors
- **Support and encourage:** “I see,” “Of course”; open body language.
- **Validate:** “I can imagine that’s frustrating.”
- **Reflect and summarize:** “It sounds like you’re saying…”
- **Clarify and dig deeper:** “Why do you think that happened?”
- **Ask open questions:** “How are you feeling about the project?”
- **Offer help:** “Is there anything I can do to help?” / “What would make this manageable?”

## 4. Key Questions to Ask Yourself
- How is the other person feeling? What is on their mind?
- What do they care about, and what do they already understand?
- What is at stake for them?
- How might their perspective affect what is possible in this interaction?

## 5. Workplace Example: Noah and Aaliyah
- Noah initially focused on his own concerns about deadlines and the transformation.
- He shifted by asking Aaliyah how she was handling her workload and listened.
- This helped ease her anxiety and build a constructive working relationship.
- Aaliyah also showed empathy by asking Noah what else he wanted to discuss.
- **Lesson:** small actions—asking, listening, and creating space—can build trust.

## 6. Handling Last-Minute Requests
When expanding Maryam’s analysis scope, Noah’s empathetic response was to:
1. Acknowledge the extra work and ask how the team is doing.
2. Listen to concerns about timelines and competing priorities.
3. Discuss a manageable timeline together.
4. Include Maryam in future key discussions.
5. Ask what else is on her mind.

**Lesson:** Empathy means considering workload, risks, inclusion, and prior experiences—not simply assuming a request is acceptable.

## 7. Empathy vs. Sympathy
Four qualities of empathy:
- **Perspective taking:** recognize another person’s perspective as their truth.
- **Staying out of judgment.**
- **Recognizing emotions** in others.
- **Communicating understanding.**

Empathy is a vulnerable choice: connect with the feeling rather than trying to fix the situation immediately.

Avoid “At least…” or forced silver linings. In difficult conversations, a response such as *“I don’t know what to say. I’m glad you told me”* can create connection.

## 8. Shared Experiences and Empathy
The cited research described in the source suggests:
- Social stress around strangers can inhibit empathy.
- A brief shared activity (15 minutes of playing a video game together) reduced this barrier in the reported study.
- Shared experiences may help people move from unfamiliarity toward connection.

## Key Takeaways
- **Empathy:** put yourself in the other person’s shoes before interacting.
- **Active listening:** validate, summarize, clarify, and ask open questions.
- Prioritize connection over assumptions or quick fixes.
- Empathy helps build trust-based relationships and more inclusive, engaging conversations.
