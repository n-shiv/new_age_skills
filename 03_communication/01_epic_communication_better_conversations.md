# EPIC Communication & Better Conversations

## 1. EPIC Communication Framework
Use EPIC to prepare for any communication.

| Element | Focus | Key question |
|---|---|---|
| **E — Empathy** | Understand audience perspective, feelings, pressures and needs | What do they care about? |
| **P — Purpose** | Define the objective and desired outcome | What should happen by the end? |
| **I — Insight** | Select the most relevant evidence and ideas | Which insights serve the purpose? |
| **C — Conversation** | Plan an engaging two-way interaction | How will I open, involve and close? |

**Tip:** Avoid assumptions about the audience. Communication should be a conversation, not a monologue.

## 2. Conversation Quality Drives Outcomes
**Quality of conversations → Quality of decisions → Quality of outcomes**

Strong conversations help teams:
- Identify risks early.
- Challenge assumptions.
- Make better decisions.
- Encourage people to speak openly.
- Inspire ambitious goals.

## 3. Barriers to Speaking Up
| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| Disagreement means disloyalty | Respectful dissent can protect the organization |
| Criticizing an idea criticizes the person | Evaluate ideas on their merits |
| Disagreeing with the group means not being a team player | Diverse perspectives improve team decisions |

**Key lesson:** Silence is not necessarily agreement. Create an environment where uncomfortable truths are heard and considered.

## 4. Techniques for Better Decisions
### Independent deliberation
- Ask people to form and write down their recommendations and reasoning before group discussion.
- Helps reduce senior-leader influence and groupthink.
- Encourages independent views.

### Devil’s Advocate / Red Team
- Assign a group to challenge a proposed plan.
- Identify assumptions, weaknesses, risks and alternatives.
- Purpose is to strengthen the proposal—not dismiss it.

## 5. Conversations: Prevent Failure and Inspire
- **Risk-focused conversations:** Encourage dissent and test alternatives to prevent poor decisions.
- **Inspiring conversations:** Communicate a shared, ambitious goal that mobilizes people.

Examples in the source: Challenger launch, Bay of Pigs, Cuban Missile Crisis and the Moon mission.

## 6. Workplace Application
When a senior person proposes an approach:
1. Independently assess feasibility and assumptions.
2. Identify limitations and alternatives.
3. Present concerns with facts.
4. Invite others’ perspectives.
5. Challenge the idea respectfully—not the person.

Example phrasing:
> “I understand the rationale. I see a potential limitation in the current assumptions. Could we evaluate an alternative before finalizing?”

## Quick Revision
- **EPIC:** Empathy → Purpose → Insight → Conversation.
- **Empathy:** Understand audience context; make no assumptions.
- **Purpose:** Define the outcome, not just an update.
- **Insight:** Share only what supports the objective.
- **Conversation:** Plan a two-way discussion.
- **Independent deliberation:** Form views before group discussion.
- **Red Team:** Deliberately test ideas for risks and weaknesses.
- **Psychological safety:** Make it safe to express disagreement.
- **Core message:** Great conversations enable better decisions and outcomes.
