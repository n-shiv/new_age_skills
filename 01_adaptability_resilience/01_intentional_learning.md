# Intentional Learning — Quick Revision Notes

## 1. Why Set Learning Intentions?
- **Learning intention** = clear purpose for what you want to learn and why.
- Helps cut through distractions → focus attention and effort.
- Makes learning more effective, enjoyable, and meaningful.
- Helps you stay motivated when progress is difficult; adjust your approach when needed.

**Common mistake:** Starting to learn without deciding what you want to gain from it.

## 2. Learning Intention vs. Performance Goal

| Learning intention | Performance goal |
|---|---|
| Focus: learning and skill-building | Focus: result or measurable target |
| Useful when the task or skill is new | Useful when you already have the required skills |
| Explore, practise, experiment, improve | Achieve, deliver, win, reach a target |
| Example: Learn a new project-management tool | Example: Deliver the project 10% faster |

**Rule of thumb:** Learn first → perform later. When you are part-way through developing a skill, use a mix of both.

## 3. How to Frame a Strong Learning Intention

### A. Focus on the journey
- Prioritize the learning process, not just the final result.
- Explore, experiment, practise, and improve.
- Avoid judging early progress only by outcomes.
- Focus on learning quality → stronger persistence and engagement.

### B. Link learning to personal meaning
- Ask: **Why does this matter to me?**
- Connect learning to personal values, interests, people, or a meaningful outcome.
- Personal meaning → autonomy, interest, energy, and intrinsic motivation.
- A meaningful purpose makes difficult learning feel worthwhile.

**Example:** Learn how to understand equipment maintenance so the team can make informed decisions and reduce work-related stress.

## 4. Writing Your Learning Intention

Use this simple structure:

> I want to **[explore / practise / understand / develop]** how to **[skill or capability]** because **[personal reason / why it matters]**.

Keep it:
- Personally meaningful
- Focused on learning, not just achievement
- Clear enough to guide your next actions

## 5. The 3 × 3 × 3 Development-Goal Framework

A simple structure for turning learning intentions into action:

| Element | What to do | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| **3 goals** | Choose three clear development goals | Keeps focus without spreading effort too thin |
| **3 months** | Work towards them over a defined period | Creates urgency, practice time, and a review point |
| **3 people** | Involve three people for support and accountability | Provides feedback, encouragement, and follow-through |

**Remember:** The numbers are a practical guide—not rigid rules. The important part is having a focused plan, a timeframe, and people who support your progress.

## 6. Intentional Learning: Two Key Mindsets

### Growth mindset
- Abilities can improve through effort, practice, and learning.
- Mistakes = feedback and learning opportunities.
- Replace fixed labels with development-focused thinking.

| Fixed mindset | Growth mindset |
|---|---|
| "I'm bad at presentations." | "I need more practice presenting." |
| "I'm not ready to lead." | "I need more leadership experience." |

### Curiosity mindset
- Ask questions; explore unfamiliar topics.
- Stay open to new ideas and perspectives.
- Make connections across different subjects.
- Curiosity starts learning and sustains motivation.

**Ways to build curiosity:** Face fears, try new experiences, meet different people, explore interests beyond your role.

## 7. Five Core Practices of Effective Learners

### 1. Set small, clear goals
- Choose goals that matter to you.
- Make them specific and actionable.
- Connect them to a real challenge or opportunity.
- Treat unique opportunities as valuable learning experiences.

### 2. Protect time for learning
- Review priorities, roles, time, and energy.
- Remove low-value activities and distractions.
- Create focused time and a suitable workspace.
- Experiment with routines; adapt what does not work.

### 3. Actively seek actionable feedback
- Tell others what skill you are working on.
- Ask for specific examples, not vague opinions.
- Treat feedback as useful data—not a personal judgment.
- Decide thoughtfully what to act on.
- Learn from people with relevant expertise.

### 4. Practise deliberately
- Repetition alone does not guarantee improvement.
- Focus on a specific skill gap.
- Practise just beyond your current ability.
- Try → receive feedback → refine → try again.
- Choose the right level of challenge: not too easy, not too difficult.

### 5. Reflect regularly
Reflection = reviewing your thinking, actions, and learning.

| When? | What to consider |
|---|---|
| **Before** | Plan: What is the task? How will I approach it? |
| **During** | Monitor: What is working? What should I adjust? |
| **After** | Review: What went well? What did I learn? What will I change next time? |

- Builds self-awareness and confidence.
- Helps identify learning gaps and improve future decisions.
- Makes change feel more familiar and manageable.
- Schedule reflection so it becomes a habit.

## 8. Key Terms
- **Intrinsic motivation:** Doing something because it is personally interesting or meaningful—not because of a reward or pressure.
- **Self-efficacy:** Belief that your actions can help you achieve a desired outcome.
- **Metacognition:** Thinking about and evaluating your own thinking and learning.
- **Deliberate practice:** Focused practice designed to improve a specific skill gap.

## Quick Takeaways
- Set an intention before starting to learn: **what, and why?**
- New skill? Start with a **learning intention**. Skilled and ready to deliver? Set a **performance goal**.
- Focus on the journey and connect learning to personal meaning.
- Use **3 × 3 × 3**: three goals, three months, three support people.
- Build a **growth mindset** and stay curious.
- Practise the five habits: **Goals → Protect time → Feedback → Deliberate practice → Reflection**.

**Remember:** Intentional learning turns everyday work, conversations, and challenges into opportunities to build new capabilities.
