# Mindsets & Adaptability — Quick Revision Notes

## 1. What Are Mindsets?
- **Mindsets** = beliefs and attitudes that act as filters between us and reality.
- Shape how we **interpret situations, feel, make decisions, and behave**.
- Often formed by past experiences and repeated reactions.
- Can be changed: choose the mindset that best fits the situation.

**Key takeaway:** The same situation can lead to different actions depending on the mindset you bring to it.

## 2. Why Mindsets Matter
Helpful mindsets can improve:
- Job performance
- Learning and achievement
- Well-being and stress management
- Relationships and fulfillment

**Important:** Mindsets are not simply good or bad. The aim is to recognize your current mindset and choose one that better serves the situation.

## 3. Six Mindset Shifts to Practise

| Limiting mindset | More adaptable mindset | Shift in thinking |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed** | **Growth** | "I can't do this" → "I can learn this" |
| **Expert** | **Curious** | "I should know" → "I can ask and explore" |
| **Reactive** | **Creative** | "Apply the usual fix" → "Explore better possibilities" |
| **Victim** | **Agent** | "I have no control" → "What can I influence?" |
| **Scarcity** | **Abundance** | "There isn't enough" → "Can we find a win-win?" |
| **Protection** | **Opportunity** | "How do I avoid risk?" → "What good can I create?" |

### Two additional shifts
| Limiting mindset | More adaptable mindset | Shift in thinking |
|---|---|---|
| **Certainty** | **Exploration** | "Stick to the plan" → "Plan, stay flexible, and explore" |

**Mindset reminders**
- Growth: challenges and mistakes = learning opportunities.
- Curious: ask questions; try unfamiliar approaches.
- Creative: focus on purpose; experiment with solutions.
- Agent: identify what you can influence or control.
- Abundance: look for shared gains and possibilities.
- Exploration: prepare, but remain open to a better route.
- Opportunity: focus on what you can make happen.

## 4. APR — Awareness, Pause, Reframe
A practical method to shift your mindset during or after a difficult situation.

1. **Awareness** — Notice your current thoughts, feelings, and mindset. How are they affecting your behaviour?
2. **Pause** — Create space before reacting. Breathe, centre yourself, and avoid acting on autopilot.
3. **Reframe** — Look at the situation through a more useful mindset. Ask what else is possible.

### Useful Reframing Questions
- **Fixed → Growth:** What can I learn from this challenge?
- **Expert → Curious:** What questions or new perspectives can I explore?
- **Reactive → Creative:** What bigger purpose am I solving for? What is one small step towards a better outcome?
- **Victim → Agent:** What can I influence? What resources or capabilities can I use?
- **Scarcity → Abundance:** Is there a possible win-win? What opportunity am I overlooking?
- **Protection → Opportunity:** What positive outcome could this situation create?

## 5. Using Adaptable Mindsets with a Team
- **Role-model:** Share your own mindset shifts and how you reframe challenges.
- **Use APR:** Help colleagues pause and reconsider their reactions.
- **Prime:** Ask helpful questions such as "What can we learn?" or "Where is the win-win?"
- **Reinforce:** Recognize and encourage growth, curiosity, and creative thinking in others.

## 6. The Power of "Yet"
- Replace **"I can't do this"** with **"I can't do this yet."**
- "Yet" signals that ability can develop with time, effort, and practice.
- Treat mistakes as information; understand what went wrong and adjust.
- Praise the **process**: effort, strategy, focus, persistence, and improvement—not just talent or results.
- Difficulty and effort can be signs that learning is happening.

**Remember:** Not successful yet does not mean never successful.

## 7. Scarcity Mindset — Understand the Tunnel Effect
- **Scarcity** = feeling that something important is insufficient (money, time, resources, support, etc.).
- Can create **tunnel vision**: attention becomes fixed on the immediate shortage.
- Short-term needs dominate; long-term priorities and consequences get overlooked.
- Stress and cognitive load can affect judgment, planning, and impulse control.
- Helpful response: recognize the "tunnel," pause, use reminders, and deliberately consider longer-term priorities.

**Example from the material:** Financial pressure led a person to focus on immediate household purchases while overlooking other upcoming expenses.

## Quick Takeaways
- Mindsets influence **thoughts → feelings → behaviour → outcomes**.
- Recognize limiting mindsets; deliberately shift to a more useful one.
- Use **APR: Awareness → Pause → Reframe**.
- Replace "I can't" with **"I can't yet."**
- Focus on learning, effort, strategy, and improvement—not only results.
- Under pressure, watch for scarcity-driven tunnel vision and reconnect with long-term priorities.

**Remember:** You may not control every situation, but you can often choose the mindset and response you bring to it.
