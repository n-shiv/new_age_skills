# Planning, Analysis, Synthesis & Recommendations

## 1. Final Four Steps
After defining, structuring and prioritizing the problem:

1. **Plan analyses and work**
2. **Conduct analyses**
3. **Synthesize findings**
4. **Develop recommendations**

The process is iterative: if evidence disproves a hypothesis, revisit the issue tree and test another hypothesis.

## 2. Build the Work Plan
Link every issue-tree endpoint to a clear plan:

| Element | What to define |
|---|---|
| Issue | A specific, answerable question |
| Hypothesis | The likely answer and what must be true |
| End product | The output needed from the analysis |
| Analyses | Work required to test the hypothesis |
| Sources | Data, databases and subject-matter experts |
| Timing & responsibility | Due date, owner and dependencies |

Also clarify the **burden of proof** with the decision-maker early. This avoids doing too much analysis—or discovering too late that the evidence is insufficient.

## 3. Conduct Analyses
- Use relevant qualitative and quantitative evidence.
- Keep asking: **Does this analysis help answer the problem?**
- Focus on accuracy and direction, not just numerical precision.
- Pause regularly to sanity-check results and update the work plan.
- Stop when there is enough evidence—or investigate further when findings challenge the hypothesis.

**Key distinction:** Analysis is the work; the answer is what the work establishes.

## 4. Synthesis: Find the “So What?”
- **Summary:** Restates the facts or activities.
- **Synthesis:** Combines findings into an insight and explains why they matter.

A good synthesis answers:
1. What have we learned?
2. What should we do next?

Example — SodaComp:
- Consumers want healthier options; sugar is a key concern.
- Competitors have limited healthy options, often at higher prices.
- Orange is a popular flavour.
- **Synthesis:** A low-sugar, affordable orange drink sold through existing channels could appeal to consumers and differentiate SodaComp while limiting operational changes.

To improve synthesis:
- Step back before drawing conclusions.
- State the insight and its implications clearly.
- Test it with others and refine it.
- Revisit the evidence; avoid relying only on standout findings.

## 5. Develop the Recommendation
A strong recommendation must be:

- **Appropriate:** Fits the organization and its capabilities.
- **Actionable:** Specifies actions, owners, timeline, resources and stakeholder buy-in.

Allow enough time to turn the synthesis into an executable plan. A good insight alone does not deliver impact; implementation does.

## Quick Revision
- **Work plan:** Issue → Hypothesis → End product → Analyses → Sources → Owner & timing.
- **Burden of proof:** Agree early on what evidence will be sufficient.
- **Analysis:** Test the hypothesis; check whether the work answers the problem.
- **Synthesis:** Insight + implication (“so what?”), not a summary.
- **Recommendation:** Appropriate + actionable, with ownership, buy-in and timeline.
- **Iterate:** Update hypotheses and plans when evidence changes.
